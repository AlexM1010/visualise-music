Privacy

Last updated 2026-09-18

This page covers two things: the Visualise Music desktop app, which you point at a folder of audio to get a map of what sounds like what, and the Freesound map on this website. In short: neither sends your audio, your file names or your maps to anyone, there is no account, no analytics and no tracking, and we do not receive any of your data. The app and the site still have to download things from a few other services, and those services can see your IP address when they do. Each one is listed below.

Who is responsible

Visualise Music is made by Alex Marshall, an individual based in the United Kingdom, who is the data controller for the purposes of UK data protection law. Contact: alex@visualise.music.

The desktop app

Your audio stays on your machine

The folder you add is read, analysed and drawn on your own computer. Your audio, file names, folder paths, analysis results and maps are never uploaded anywhere. Nothing is sent to us as part of normal use.

What the app downloads, and from where

To keep the installer small, the app downloads its analysis tools the first time you set it up. Every request is a plain download, and none of them includes anything about your audio. Like any download, the server can see your IP address and basic request details such as software versions.

  • Python packages, about 1–1.4 GB, from the Python Package Index (privacy notice). If you choose NVIDIA GPU support, some packages come from download.pytorch.org, run by the PyTorch Foundation (privacy policy).
  • The audio model, about 600 MB for the default model and about 780 MB for each of the two alternatives if you pick one, from Hugging Face (privacy policy). The app only goes back to Hugging Face for a model that is not already on your machine.
  • Update check. Once each time the app starts, it asks a release feed hosted on GitHub whether a newer version exists (GitHub's privacy statement). The request contains nothing about you or your library. If you are offline, the app skips it without saying anything. If you install an update, it is downloaded from the same place.

If you installed the app from the Microsoft Store, it never runs its own update check: Windows and the Store handle updates, under Microsoft's privacy statement. The first-time setup downloads above still apply.

The Support button opens Buy Me a Coffee in your web browser. Nothing is sent unless you click it, and what happens on that site falls under their privacy policy.

What the app stores on your computer

All of this is kept in the app's own folders on your machine. The app never writes to the folders you add; it only reads them.

  • Your libraries and maps: the list of folders you have added, including their full paths, plus each folder's map, analysis results and analysis cache. On Windows this is in %APPDATA%\com.visualisemusic.desktop, and on Linux in ~/.local/share/com.visualisemusic.desktop. The App data button in the app opens it.
  • Rebuildable data: the Python environment, the downloaded models, playback copies of your audio, and logs from recent runs. Logs can include file names from your library. On Windows this is in %LOCALAPPDATA%\com.visualisemusic.desktop, and on Linux in the same folder as above. It can run to several gigabytes, and deleting it only means the app will download or rebuild it.

The Windows uninstaller has a separate checkbox for each of these two folders. On Linux, or to remove things by hand, delete the folders listed above.

The Freesound map on this website

The Freesound map is a single static page hosted on GitHub Pages. It has no account, no cookies, no analytics and no tracking.

  • Hosting. Like any web host, GitHub records your IP address when you load the page (GitHub's privacy statement). We do not receive those logs.
  • Sound previews. When you play or download a sound, your browser fetches the preview file directly from Freesound, which can see your IP address (Freesound's privacy policy).
  • Signing in to Freesound (optional). To download the uploader's original files instead of previews, you can enter a Freesound API credential of your own (a client id and secret you create on freesound.org) and sign in on freesound.org itself. We never see your Freesound password. Your browser talks to Freesound directly: the credential and the access token Freesound issues are kept in your browser's local storage and are sent only to freesound.org, never to us or anyone else. Clicking originals on lets you sign out or forget the credential, and clearing this site's data removes both. You can also revoke access from your Freesound settings.
  • Your settings, such as layout, looping, tempo, key and the list of sounds you have picked, are saved in your browser's local storage so they are still there next time. They never leave your device, and clearing this site's data in your browser removes them.
  • Download button. Installers are downloaded from GitHub Releases, which falls under GitHub's privacy statement.

Legal basis and what we keep

We hold no personal data about users of the app or the website. The only personal data involved is the IP address that the services above see when your device downloads from them. The app makes those requests because it cannot work without them, and the update check keeps it secure and up to date. That is our legitimate interest in providing software that works. Each service handles your data under its own policy, linked above.

If you email us, we keep your message and email address only as long as we need them to deal with your question, and use them for nothing else.

Your rights

Under UK data protection law you can ask for access to, correction of, or deletion of personal data we hold about you, and you can object to how it is used. Email the address above to do so. If you are unhappy with the answer, you can complain to the Information Commissioner's Office.

Changes

If the app or the site starts handling data differently, this page will be updated before that version is released, and the date at the top will change.
